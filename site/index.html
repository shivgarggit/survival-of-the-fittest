How to read this

Two returns, not one

Time-weighted return ignores when money went in and answers "how good were the rules". Money-weighted return (IRR) keeps the timing in and answers "how would my money actually have done". Turn on contributions and the two diverge; the gap is entirely down to timing.

How a quarter runs

On the first trading day the book is rebuilt at equal weight and held untouched until the last. Departures are sold in full, arrivals bought fresh, survivors trimmed or topped up. Contributions arriving mid-quarter are bought straight into the current book.

What is not modelled

No commission, bid-ask spread, slippage or tax is deducted anywhere. A real account running this would return less than what you see here, and the gap widens the more often you contribute.

Selection is an input

The roster for each quarter is a decision, not a formula this page can verify. If a name was chosen using anything that would not have been knowable on the rebalance date, every figure here is flattered.

Leverage inside the basket

UPRO is a 3× daily leveraged S&P fund holding an equal slot in most quarters. It carries roughly three times its weight in market exposure, so part of both the return and the drawdown is leverage rather than stock picking.

This is a backtest

It describes what a set of rules would have produced on historical data. It is not a track record of money actually managed, not a forecast, and not investment advice.
